The story

A stranger in a country at war.

In 1579, a man from Africa walked into Japan and changed history. His name was Yasuke.

Taken from his homeland and carried across oceans by forces beyond his control, Yasuke arrives in a Japan consumed by war. The country is fractured. Daimyō wage endless battles for power. Samurai live and die by the sword. And at the center of the chaos stands the most feared man in Japan: Oda Nobunaga.

What begins as curiosity soon becomes something far more dangerous. Nobunaga sees in Yasuke not a servant… but a warrior. As kingdoms burn and armies collide, Yasuke rises from outsider to samurai, standing beside one of the most powerful men Japan has ever known.

But power always demands a price.

As war spreads across the nation, Yasuke is forced to confront impossible questions. What does loyalty become when it is soaked in blood? Can a man ever truly belong in a world determined to make him a symbol? And who are you when every nation, every empire, every master tries to decide your identity for you?

Blending brutal Sengoku-era warfare with intimate human emotion, this sweeping historical epic reimagines the life of the legendary African samurai with cinematic intensity and profound humanity.

This is not the story of a weapon. It is the story of a man fighting to remain himself in a world determined to turn him into something else.

For readers of Shōgun, Musashi, and epic historical fiction rooted in real history, honor, identity, war, and survival.

About the author

Cliff St. Hubert

Cliff St. Hubert is a Miami-based writer, visual artist and television producer at Island TV. He writes historical and speculative fiction about people history tried to overlook, and brings a producer's eye for the cinematic to every page.

He is also the founder of 3rd I AI, a creative studio built for independent storytellers and diaspora creators. His novels include The Man They Called Yasuke, the result of years of research and writing, and The Last Prophet of Gaza.
